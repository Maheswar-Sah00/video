# What If You Stopped Drinking Water? — build contract (read fully before writing code)

77-second vertical reel (1080×1920, 30 fps, **2310 frames**), Remotion 4, everything drawn in code
(React + inline SVG). No images from the internet, no AI video. There is no storyboard: this doc is
the brief. Story told to "YOU" (second person): an ordinary young man (him = you) takes his last
sip of water — ever — and we follow his body hour by hour, day by day, until it stops (~3 days),
then REWIND, he drinks, and the reel ends on "Go drink a glass of water."

**Look:** cinematic **anime-cel 2D** for HIM and his world (kitchen, desk, bathroom mirror, hot
street, living room, bedroom): bold clean ink outlines (`C.ink`, 2–5 px, round joins, varied
width), flat cel fills with 1–2 hard-edged shadow tones + soft gradient light and a coloured RIM
light, painterly light. Every body / organ / cell shot is **glossy medical illustration**: the body
as translucent **x-ray glass** (pale blue outlines `C.bodyGhost*`, faint skeleton) on deep navy
`C.medBg`, organs glossy with specular highlights, glowing aqua WATER inside; micro shots = glossy
cells with depth (big soft foreground cells, sharp mid, small dim background). See
`docs/ref/hero-s01.png` + `hero-s14.png` for the character in the previous reel and
`out/stills/lead-cells-f0000.png` for the micro vocabulary. NOT photoreal, NOT 3D, NOT horror.

Title (global overlay, abs 10–150, y≈240–760): "WHAT IF YOU STOPPED / DRINKING WATER? / FOR THE

## Transitions (engine in src/Transition.tsx; your job: compose ends/starts so they read)

* **zoomIn → (fx,fy)**: OUTGOING scales up 1→2.6× around (fx,fy) with growing blur (stays opaque).
  Put the thing we dive into exactly at (fx,fy) and push your own camera toward it in the last ~20
  frames. INCOMING grows from 0.7× out of a soft ellipse at the centre — open on that thing.
* **zoomOut (fx,fy)**: OUTGOING shrinks toward (fx,fy) into a soft ellipse and fades; INCOMING
  starts at 2.1× and settles to 1× (the camera pulling back) — keep its centre interesting.
* **fade**: plain crossfade — make the two compositions rhyme (similar masses).
* **whip**: whip pan — old scene flies out left, new one in from the right, motion-blurred.
* **rack**: rack focus — old scene blurs out, new one sharpens as it fades up.
* **xray / xrayUp**: a glowing pale-cyan scan line sweeps DOWN (xray) / UP (xrayUp) the frame
  revealing the incoming scene.
* **slideUp**: camera tilts DOWN (outgoing moves up out of frame, incoming comes from the bottom).
  **slideDown**: camera tilts UP (outgoing moves down, incoming comes from the top).
* **clock** (NEW — the time jumps "Day one / two / three"): a glowing clock hand sweeps once round
  the centre (540,960) from 12 o'clock clockwise with a faint 12-tick dial, revealing the incoming
  scene behind it. Keep your centre busy; the HUD's DAY badge pops at the same moment.
* **flash (color)**: a short bright flash covers the cut. Make your last frames brighten toward it.
* **dip**: the outgoing scene fades to black in the first half, the incoming fades up from black.

  translucent outline + faint inner anatomy) on navy, glossy organs with highlights and inner glow,
  aqua water glowing inside; micro = glossy cells with depth of field (blurred big foreground cells
  — a small blur on a small group is fine, or softer/lighter flat versions).
* Colours: `src/lib/palette.ts` ONLY. **Reserved meanings, never mixed up:** aqua = WATER ·
  white-silver cubes = SALT · bright red = BLOOD · coral-peach = his BODY CELLS (plump → shrivelled)
  · violet-magenta capsule = ADH hormone · electric yellow = the THIRST ALARM / nerve signals ·
  straw → amber → brown = URINE colour · murky olive-green = TOXINS · orange-red glow = HEAT /
  overheating and pain throbs (`C.heat`, `C.pain`) · pale x-ray blue = the glass body outline ·
  mint-teal = RECOVERY (S19–S20 only). Use the Cells components for every cell / molecule.
* **EVERYTHING MOVES — the most important requirement:** every shot needs **≥3 independently
  moving layers + a camera move** (drift/push/dolly/tilt/roll/parallax), with different speeds per
  layer, and **anticipation, follow-through and inertia** (overshoot + settle, hair and clothes
  lagging, eases not linear). Never a still illustration with a slow zoom. Water sloshes and
  glints, molecules drift in 2–3 depth layers, vessels pulse with flowing red cells, light shifts
  (sunbeams, dust motes, caustics); he breathes and blinks, hair stirs, curtains move, heat haze
  wobbles. Pseudo-depth: foreground layers bigger, faster, softer; background smaller, slower,
  dimmer.
* Labels: `<Label/>` from lib, 1–4 words, appear exactly when the thing is visible AND named in the
  narration (time them to the words — `wordAt('n06','Thirst')`), gone soon after. Never
  paragraphs. ≤ 3 labels per scene.
* `<BoilDefs/>` line boil on the character / line art for a hand-animated feel (on groups, not
  full-screen fills — it's expensive).
* **No gore, no horror, dignity.** No vomit, no blood, no corpse, no skull imagery, no flatline
  cliché, no hospital drama. No toilets / urine streams: urine is shown only as the colour CHART
  and a glowing bladder in the x-ray body. His end (S16) is gentle: tired, eyes closing, the
  evening light fading — he simply drifts off.
* Photosensitivity: no strobing — any flicker/flash ≤ ~3 per second; the dizziness spin is smooth.
* Performance: aim < 1 s/frame. Avoid feGaussianBlur on big areas (use radial gradients / layered
  strokes for glows; small blur on small groups is OK), keep element counts sane (< ~1500 nodes),
  memoise static geometry with `useMemo`. Many particles: draw them as ONE path where you can.

## Scientific accuracy (non-negotiable)
* An adult is ~60 % water (≈ 42 L in a 70 kg person): ~2/3 inside the cells, ~1/3 outside (blood
  plasma + the fluid between cells). We lose ≈ 2.5 L a day: urine ~1.5 L, sweat + skin ~0.5 L,
  breath ~0.4 L, the rest in stool. (Food normally provides ~20 % of our water — in our story he
  takes in NO water at all.)
* With no intake the blood plasma gets more concentrated (saltier: sodium / osmolality up) →
  sensor cells (osmoreceptors) in the **hypothalamus** shrink → **thirst**; the **pituitary**
  (just below it) releases **ADH** (vasopressin) → in the **kidneys** water is pulled back from the
  urine into the blood (collecting ducts) → little urine, **dark** amber.
* ~1–2 % loss: thirst, dry sticky mouth (less saliva), headache, poorer concentration.
* Blood volume falls → the blood is **thicker** (more red cells per litre, more viscous) → the
  heart beats **faster** to keep up (75 → 110+ bpm); standing up drops the blood pressure
  (orthostatic hypotension) → dizziness.
* Water moves OUT of the cells by **osmosis** into the saltier blood → cells **shrivel** —
  including brain cells: the **brain shrinks** measurably (pulls slightly away from the skull) →
  confusion, foggy thinking, irritability, later delirium.
* Skin loses its elasticity (a pinched fold stays tented), eyes sink, lips crack. **Sweating
  falls** → the body can't cool itself → body temperature **rises** (overheating).
* Severe (~10 %+): the **kidneys fail** (acute kidney injury) → urine stops, wastes (urea) and
  potassium build up in the blood ("poisons") → the heart's rhythm becomes **irregular**; the blood
  pressure **collapses** (hypovolemic shock) → unconsciousness → death.
* Typical survival without any water: **about 3 days** (range ~2–7 days depending on heat,
  activity, health). The S17 card says "≈ 3 DAYS" + "2–7 days, depending on heat & activity".
